## 6. Risk Analysis

Would a syndicate approve this staking strategy? **The staking itself, yes. The market/odds selection feeding into it, no.**

- Flat 1-unit (10/10) staking across nearly every bet is textbook-correct discipline and the single most professional thing in this dataset.
- The problem isn't bet *sizing*, it's bet *selection variance*. A flat-staked portfolio that includes 51 bets at odds 5.00+ with a 4% hit rate has enormous variance baked in regardless of stake size — you're taking lottery-ticket-shaped risk with steady-bettor-shaped staking, and the two don't match. A syndicate would flag this as an exposure problem, not a staking problem: your bankroll's volatility is being driven by tail bets that contribute almost nothing positive in expectation.
- 705 of your bets (everything under 5.00 odds) collectively lose about -3.6% yield — survivable, refinable. The 51 bets at 5.00+ are what actually threatens bankroll ruin over a long enough sample, because a bad month like July (20-bet losing streak in it) is exactly what high-variance long-shot clusters produce.

- **Markets to avoid entirely:** anything at odds ≥5.00 regardless of market type; 3+ leg combos; Over-corners specifically; Cards and Double Chance (no evidence of skill, tiny sample, terrible results).
- **Leagues to specialize in:** the "second tier of strong footballing nations" pattern from earlier advice actually shows up here — Japan, Eng. - **Leagues to remove or heavily cut:** Serie A, Premier League, La Liga. If you can't fully cut them, at minimum cap your odds ceiling in these leagues at 3.00 and see if that alone fixes the yield.
- **Ideal odds range:** 1.80–2.99, with 2.30–2.99 as your highest-confidence zone. Treat anything above 3.00 as requiring extra justification, and anything above 5.00 as requiring you to sit out.
- **Maximum bets per day:** your data doesn't have daily granularity, but at 756 picks over ~205 days that's roughly 3.7 picks/day on average — likely too many for a one-person process to genuinely value-check each one, given how poorly the numbers hold up. Cutting the low-conviction "filler" markets (half-time, cards, one-off match results) would naturally cut volume toward something more sustainable, like 2-3 well-checked picks/day.
- **Bankroll strategy:** keep flat staking. It's the one part of your process that's already professional-grade.
- **Record-keeping improvement:** start logging odds-at-close alongside odds-at-bet, even manually, for a sample of your bets. Right now you have no way to measure whether you have real predictive edge or are just riding market-accurate pricing — CLV tracking would answer that question directly, which nothing else in this dataset can.
