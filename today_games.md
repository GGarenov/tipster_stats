## STEP 3-6 — Team Analysis, Team News, Tactics, Situational Factors (condensed)

### Cottbus – Greuther Fürth
- **Form**: Cottbus 1W-1L in league (3-1 home vs Hannover, 0-3 away at
  Bielefeld) + cup L0-2 vs Bundesliga side Augsburg. Fürth 0W-1D-1L (1-1 at
  St Pauli, 2-4 home vs Nürnberg) + cup L1-2 vs Bochum.
- **Goals/xG (n=2 each, low-confidence)**: Cottbus 1.5 GF/1.5 GA... actually
  2.0 GA/g; xGF **1.07 — reportedly the worst attacking xG in the entire
  2. Bundesliga**. Fürth 1.5 GF/2.5 GA/g; xGA **2.15 away — reportedly the
  worst away defensive xG in the league**; Cottbus's own home xGA is
  reportedly the league's worst too (2.83).
- **Team news**: Cottbus missing top striker Engelhardt (serious knee
  injury, was 22-goal scorer last season) — replaced by unproven new
  signing Bogdanov. Fürth missing captain Hrgota (transferred out), Müller
  (illness), Wallentowitz (injury).
- **Read**: two genuinely leaky defenses on paper (small sample, but both
  independently flagged as league-worst in their split), both missing a
  key player. Goal-environment case is *plausible* but the sample backing
  it is two games each.

### Heidenheim – Dynamo Dresden
- **Form**: Heidenheim W4-3 vs Osnabrück, L1-4 at Hertha, plus a historic
  cup humiliation (L2-5 to 4th-tier Jeddeloh II). Dresden L0-1 vs Nürnberg,
  W1-0 vs Darmstadt, cup W6-0 (weak opposition).
- **Goals/xG**: Heidenheim conceding heavily early (3.5 GA/g, xGA 2.21) —
  and this matches a real **multi-season pattern**: 2.12 GA/game across
  their full relegation season in the Bundesliga last year (34-game
  sample, much more trustworthy than this year's n=2). Dresden tighter
  (1.5 GA/g, xGA 1.38) and had a genuinely good underlying 2025/26 (71
  scored/40 conceded across 34 games, one of the league's best goal
  differences despite only an 11th-place finish).
- **Team news**: Dresden missing **4 players including Daferner, their
  joint-2nd top scorer last season (18 goals)**, plus starting keeper
  Grill (backup starts). Heidenheim's coach Schmidt was suspended for the
  cup tie; unclear if the ban carries to this league match. Heidenheim in
  a documented "crisis" narrative post-cup upset (coach's own words: "a
  huge backpack").
- **Read**: Heidenheim's leaky defense is a real, multi-season signal, not
  just noise — genuine reason to lean toward goals being conceded. But
  Dresden is missing their main scoring threat, which caps their own
  output. Net effect on total goals is genuinely ambiguous.

### Karlsruhe – Wolfsburg
- **Form**: KSC unbeaten in league (W2-1 vs Bielefeld, D0-0 at
  Kaiserslautern) + cup W2-1. Wolfsburg unbeaten too (W1-0 at Hannover,
  D0-0 vs Kaiserslautern) but needed penalties to escape a 3-3 cup draw
  against amateur side VSG Altglienicke — "conceded three goals against
  amateur opposition," a real defensive-solidity flag despite 2 clean
  sheets in their 2 league games.
- **Goals/xG**: Both teams' actual scoring is very low so far (combined,
  only **1 goal conceded between them across 4 league matches**) — but
  both show much healthier underlying **xGF (KSC 2.13, Wolfsburg 1.91)**
  than their actual output, suggesting under-finishing/luck rather than a
  genuinely low-scoring identity. Last season: Wolfsburg conceded 2.03/g
  in the Bundesliga (leaky at the top level, echo of the Heidenheim
  pattern for relegated sides).
- **Team news**: KSC has thin centre-back depth (only 3 recognised
  options) but no confirmed injuries beyond return-from-injury updates.
  Wolfsburg's reported injury list (Gerhardt, Bernhardsson, Svanberg,
  Rexhbeçaj) came from a source that **also mislabeled two Wolfsburg
  players as Karlsruhe's** — treat with real caution. Wolfsburg's coach
  Strobl is their 5th in 15 months, leading a squad worth ~4x the next
  most valuable 2. Bundesliga club (€169M) — financial gap says one thing,
  squad-gelling risk says another.
- **Tactics**: Wolfsburg explicitly set up in a compact 5-4-1/5-3-2 low
  block defensively — consistent with their low-scoring, low-conceding
  start. KSC rested 8 days vs Wolfsburg's 5 (plus extra-time fatigue from
  the cup).
- **Read**: underlying quality (xG, financial gap) points to more goals
  and a Wolfsburg edge than the raw scoreline/table suggests; situational
  factors (rest, home advantage, low-block tactics) cut the other way.
  Genuinely a coin-flip case.

### Nürnberg – Bielefeld
- **Form**: Nürnberg 2-0 in league by a combined 7-2, plus a cup shootout
  win. Emphatic start. Bielefeld 1-1 in league (W3-0 vs Cottbus, L1-2 at
  KSC), **eliminated from the cup by a 3. Liga side on penalties**, and
  had a player sent off (second yellow) in that cup match — a real
  suspension risk for this game if the ban carries into league play
  (standard under German football's disciplinary system).
- **Goals/xG**: Nürnberg scoring heavily (3.5 GF/g) with a real tactical
  fit — an explicit preview source flagged Bielefeld's new high-press
  defensive scheme as "risky against Nürnberg's pace/counter-attacking
  threat." Bielefeld, despite only 4 goals scored, has reportedly
  generated **the highest cumulative xG of any club in the league so far
  (5.7 through 2 matches)** — they're under-converting, which is actually
  a bullish signal for their own scoring going forward, not a bearish one.
- **Team news**: Nürnberg lost a starting CB (Lochoshvili) and fielded 3
  new, unproven defensive signings — some integration risk despite the
  good start. Bielefeld lost captain Corboz, has a new coach (Kirch,
  early tenure), and the Felix suspension risk above.
- **Read**: this is the most one-sided situational picture of the four
  matches — in-form, tactically-favoured home side with a clear style
  advantage vs a team that just got knocked out by a lower-tier side,
  possibly down a defender, with a defensive scheme its own tactical
  profile is described as vulnerable to counters.

effectively a straight match-odds bet with no push possibility. My
estimate (~48% Wolfsburg win) sits close enough to the market's own
implied number (~47.5% devigged) that there's no confident edge either
way — the financial-quality gap that would argue for backing Wolfsburg is
offset by real situational drag (less rest, cup-match fatigue, new coach,

## STEP 10 — Red Flags (why I'm not more excited about anything here)

- **Every single match is n=2 league games per team.** This is exactly
  the sample size the brief itself said not to trust. Every number above
  should be read as provisional, not settled.
- **Bet365 prices were not obtainable in this session for any of the four
  matches** — every price used came from a secondary book or
  odds-aggregator (ODDSET, DraftKings, tipico-linked previews,
  Sportwetten24). No opening-line or line-movement data was found
  anywhere, so I cannot tell whether any of these prices have already
  been corrected by sharper money.
- **Source reliability problems showed up repeatedly**: a garbled,
  internally-impossible odds figure (Cottbus-Fürth), an injury list that
  mislabeled Wolfsburg players as Karlsruhe's, conflicting KSC 2025/26
  goals data across sources, conflicting kickoff-time reports (resolved,
  but not without friction). I've discarded anything that looked
  internally inconsistent, but it's a sign the underlying data quality
  this week is below what I'd want for real money.
- **Advanced stats (shots, shots on target, big chances) were essentially
  unavailable everywhere** — FBref, Sofascore, and WhoScored were blocked
  across all four research passes. Every xG figure used comes from a
  single source (FootyStats) with no independent cross-check.
- **Squad/coach turnover is heavy across the board**: Wolfsburg's entire
  squad rebuilt post-relegation under a 5th coach in 15 months; Heidenheim
  post-relegation and in a self-described "crisis"; Bielefeld and Cottbus
  both under new coaches. None of last year's data transfers cleanly onto
  this year's group of players.
- **The one modestly-positive EV pick (Nürnberg Over 2.5) still rests on
  a single-sourced price and a sample of two games** — the qualitative
  case (tactical mismatch, suspension risk, cup-loss morale hit for
  Bielefeld) is genuinely convergent and independently corroborated (a
  preview source flagged the tactical vulnerability without prompting,
  and FootyStats' own separate model landed even higher, at 80% Over 2.5,
  than my more conservative 67%) — but "convergent qualitative signal on
  thin quantitative data" is a real category of value, not a certainty.
